## What was directly inspected

Review scope: six downloaded public videos, sampled at 3-second intervals from the beginning through the last interval before the end. All nonblank samples on their contact sheets were inspected. Audio meaning came from YouTube auto captions for Welch and Animathions' difference of squares; local Whisper base.en supplied the other four transcripts because caption retrieval was unavailable/rate-limited. The circular-motion ASR returned music only; the derivative ASR returned song fragments rather than mathematical narration. Such recognition is not a reliable music/lyrics assessment. These are sampled visual + text/audio-track analyses, not continuous playback, sound-mix evaluation or frame-accurate motion QA. Timestamps below locate observed samples and nearby transcript passages, not exact cut boundaries.

### 1. A circle emerges from straight-line motions

[MindYourDecisions, 23 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/T4q5Sl8SYsc). At 00:00, an orange point sits on a blue horizontal diameter inside a circle. At 00:03 and 00:06, additional constrained points and differently oriented diameters appear. By 00:12-00:15, the accumulated points visibly outline a smaller moving circle. At 00:18-00:21, the construction continues with an Al-Tusi attribution beneath it.

**Concept:** emergence from simple constraints. **Principle manifestation:** one persistent object system makes each addition meaningful; the spectacle is the relationship itself. This supplies a low-verbal-entry experience and repeated partial reveals. It does not explicitly teach the underlying theorem or provide transfer, and the sampled opening has no question/prediction prompt. Its large count is a useful counterexample to imposing narration, a quiz, or a complete educational explanation on every visual format. For our SAT lessons, preserve the immediate visible relationship but add the reasoning the lesson promises.

### 2. Apparent free chocolate, then the hidden change is exposed

[MindYourDecisions, 48 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/6aDB9VLnyZQ). At 00:00, a 5-by-5 chocolate grid and cut lines give a familiar, countable object. At 00:03-00:09, rearrangement apparently restores the grid while freeing a piece; the displayed arithmetic contradicts ordinary subtraction. At 00:15-00:21, the demonstration repeats. At 00:24, the explanation begins. At 00:27-00:36, colored diagonal strips mark the concealed added area; at 00:39-00:45, those strips are compared with the extra square.

**Concept:** conservation paradox resolved by exposing a hidden change. **Principle manifestation:** recognizable premise -> failed expectation -> visible diagnostic -> restored consistency. The first apparent result is a partial payoff; the ending owes the correction. The animation deliberately adds area during the illusion and subsequently discloses that fact. Do not imitate the first half as an honest area-preserving rearrangement. SAT adaptation: show why an apparently identical silhouette can hide changed dimensions, then explicitly measure the discrepancy.

### 3. Same creator, a typical-count circle-area explanation

[MindYourDecisions, 57 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/feHYG4Bm8jA), 117,251 views. The observed 195-entry channel catalogue has a rounded median of 117,000 views. At 00:00-00:06, a blue circle and radius are already visible, but the main image changes little. At 00:09-00:15, concentric rings appear and begin opening. At 00:18-00:24, the unwrapped segments resolve into a triangle. At 00:30-00:36, the base `2πr`, height `r`, and area calculation appear. At 00:45-00:54, a mathematical-paper image supplies a source coda.

**Concept:** reinterpret an unfamiliar area as a familiar triangle. **Principle manifestation:** stable object identity, visible correspondence and a formula earned by the construction. This aligns strongly with the theory while performing far below the two exceptional MYD references. A plausible hypothesis is that the paradox/emergence entries offer more immediate surprise, whereas this starts with a familiar topic and historical context. That is a testable interpretation, not an explanation established by views. The videos differ in age, subject and distribution. The smooth ring-unwrapping picture is an illustration whose limiting argument requires care; the video itself mentions a rigorous justification.

### 4. A model becomes simpler when the reference point changes

[Welch Labs, 59 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/KpmrQdaQjGg). At 00:00-00:12, Earth, circular constructions and Mars' traced path occupy the same frame. Around 00:15-00:21, a direction cue accompanies the retrograde loop. At 00:27-00:30, geocentric and heliocentric diagrams appear as an explicit comparison. At 00:33-00:45, the vectors and reference points are visible within the construction. Around 00:48-00:51, the simplified orbit becomes inspectable. The final sampled frames contain a link-to-full-video CTA after the explanation.

**Concept:** change coordinates to expose an invariant/equivalence. **Principle manifestation:** the diagram carries the explanation, using direct comparisons and cancellation instead of merely asserting a new model. Transfer to SAT: two apparently different equations or graphs can describe the same relationship after a stated transformation. Be precise about model assumptions; a rendered circular construction is not a proof of every astronomical claim. This subset has no directly reviewed lower Welch comparator, so it cannot explain Welch's relative performance.

### 5. Small-channel algebra versus calculus comparison

[Animathions difference of squares, 38 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/LY4lM_5y7d8): 1,834 views, 64 likes, published 2026-03-09. Around 00:00, a question/equation opens; at 00:03-00:09, an `a`-square is built. At 00:12-00:18, the `b`-square is identified and removed. At 00:21-00:27, the remaining L-shape becomes a rectangle with sides `a-b` and `a+b`. At 00:30-00:36, the identity resolves and is boxed.

[Animathions derivative, 35 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/M3w7_ZwQ-xU): 1,033 views, 14 likes, published 2026-03-14. Around 00:00, the topic question opens; at 00:03-00:09, a curve, points and coordinate labels appear. At 00:12-00:18, a secant slope triangle and quotient build. Around 00:21-00:24, the secant approaches a tangent as the increment shrinks. At 00:27-00:33, the limit definition replaces the graph and is boxed.

**Concepts:** area-preserving rearrangement versus limiting a moving secant. Both use restrained backgrounds, semantic motion and a final formula. The algebra example starts from a more familiar, manipulable object and has fewer symbolic prerequisites; the calculus example adds several labels and a limit. That makes prerequisite burden a useful candidate explanation to test. Counts do not isolate it, and two observations cannot establish a general result. The algebra entry is above the observed 14-entry catalogue's median of 1,550, but it is not that channel's maximum; the largest entry is the approximately 3,100-view tangent-circle-area video, inventoried only. Both reviewed clips have tiny essential labels in the contact-sheet view; actual phone-size legibility needs a separate playback review. For SAT, state `a >= b >= 0` for this area illustration, then distinguish it from the identity's broader algebraic validity.

## Concepts to carry into the SAT slate

| Reusable concept | What makes it useful | SAT implementation | Do not copy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apparent invariant fails | The viewer has a definite prediction to revise. | Unequal graph-axis units make an apparently slope-one line have another slope. | A fake transformation presented as valid mathematics. |
| Hidden term becomes visible | The overlooked quantity has a place in the picture. | Two strips expose the cross term in `(x+a)^2`; a removed square becomes a rectangle. | The formula appearing before the missing pieces can be inspected. |
| Reinterpret a hard object | A new relationship inherits familiar reasoning. | Circle sectors form an approximate rectangle; a shaded region is recomposed. | Claiming a limiting illustration is a finite exact proof without qualification. |
| Change the representation | An equivalent view exposes what was obscured. | Translate a parabola or rescale a coordinate system with labels retained. | Morphing an object without showing which quantities change. |
| Simple constraints create surprising structure | Each added constraint advances one visual system. | Equal increments reveal linear versus quadratic growth on a shared axis. | A complicated opening map whose individual relationships are unreadable. |

The strongest contribution of these references is a vocabulary of mathematical experiences. Their counts help choose what to inspect. They do not turn the current principles into verified distribution laws. The same catalogue contains polished-looking mathematical candidates with small counts and strong within-channel variation; the theory should predict viewer experience and learning mechanisms first, then earn any performance claim through prospective comparable tests.

## Data limitations

- Subscribers are current public channel size, usually rounded, not historical size or the number exposed to a video.
- Public views are cumulative and videos have unequal ages. Most unreviewed publication dates remain unknown in this collection.
- No private retention, engaged-view analytics, traffic sources, impressions, completion, rewatching or learning outcomes were available.
- A channel can mix animations, interviews, handwriting and republished excerpts. The CSV flags channel relevance and exact review scope; metadata-only rows require visual confirmation before being labeled animation exemplars.
- Catalogue medians are medians of the entries returned in this observation, not lifetime performance baselines and not age-adjusted controls.
- Music recognition and auto captions are imperfect. No claims about exact sound design or continuous timing were inferred from sampled stills.
