# What SAT videos go viral

Short-form research from 2026-10-27, done to plan a run of about 60 videos a day. It covers YouTube Shorts and TikTok. Instagram profiles need a login, so the Instagram evidence comes from our reference channel's own counts (see `sat-math-topics.md`).

- **V** (verified) means the number was read directly from public data on 2026-10-27.
- **R** (reported) means it comes from an article or search snippet.
- YouTube IDs are `youtube.com/shorts/<ID>`.

**Account-level picture:**
- **Brainblast** is the dominant SAT-math Shorts account. It has 1,671 Shorts with a median of 29K views and a mean of 176K. 51 are over 1M and 422 are over 100K, and the top 1% earn 31% of its views.
- **Platform gap:** the same script peaks around 1.5M on TikTok against 13.5M on YouTube. YouTube Shorts is the larger venue for SAT math.

## Formats, ranked by evidence

1. **The trap question** ("looks easy, everyone gets it wrong").
   - **Evidence:** nearly all 51 of Brainblast's million-view Shorts use this format. Its titles containing "trick" have a median of 106K views.
   - **Structure:** the exact question in frame 1, the tempting wrong move, a "wait" beat, one insight, then the answer.
   - **Why it spreads:** comments (people post their answer and want to be right).
   - **Our version:** ink the wrong step, strike it in the accent blue, and run the one transformation that shows why.
2. **Prestige framing** ("Harvard's entry exam", "Einstein couldn't solve this").
   - **Evidence:** the highest median title pattern on Brainblast YouTube, 388.5K (n=26).
   - **Catch:** the claims are usually fabricated. Use true provenance instead ("from the 1982 SAT", "from official practice test 4").
3. **The test was wrong.**
   - **Evidence:** Veritasium's 1982 coin-rotation Short has 10.45M and MindYourDecisions has 1.27M.
   - **Why it fits:** a true story, one diagram, one transformation.
4. **One-technique hack or "cheat code".**
   - **Evidence:** the percent hack has 4.84M, the quadratic shortcut 928K, and the percent swap outside SAT 26.3M.
   - **Our version:** pair the hack with why it works. The percent swap is one rectangle read two ways.
5. **Course or unit in 60 seconds.**
   - **Evidence:** on SAT TikTok it drives saves rather than reach (a 2.9% save rate against 1.0% for trap videos). Six Trigs in 60 Seconds has 874K.
   - **On Instagram:** our reference channel's Calculus III in 60 seconds has 994K views and is its top post, so this format is worth testing hard there.
6. **Desmos hacks and speedruns.**
   - **Evidence:** Desmos-only Shorts have a median of 3.2K. The best reach 300-372K, and long-form versions do far better.
   - **Role:** a save driver, not a reach driver.
7. **Test-date content** (predictions, "how did it go?", score release).
   - **Evidence:** test-day posts get 300-500K with 4-7K shares. Predictions get 155-177K at about 3% saves.
8. **Score journeys, SAT vs ACT, admissions.**
   - **Evidence:** big numbers (1.7-5.3M).
   - **Fit:** they are personality-driven and fit our style poorly.

## Topics, ranked by presence in high-view videos

1. **Exponent rules:**
   - same-base rewriting
   - 4^x + 4^x + 4^x
   - comparing powers
   - fractional exponents
   - 32^x·8^y from a linear equation (at least 5 videos at 899K+)

   This topic is in 28 of 63 Brainblast million-plus Shorts. By far the biggest.
2. **Percents:**
   - what "per cent" means
   - percent of a percent
   - the swap (a% of b = b% of a)
   - reverse percent
3. **Geometry angles and shaded areas:** angle sums, polygon angles, the triangle inequality.
4. **Radicals:** like terms (√50 + √8), infinite nested roots.
5. **Units-digit cycles:** number sense, rarely on the real SAT, but huge reach.
6. **Clever systems:** using x + y and xy without solving.
7. **Quadratics:** sum of roots, discriminant, factoring.
8. **Number of solutions:** no solution and infinitely many.

Rare among high-view Shorts are statistics, regressions, circle equations and trig. They show up in save-heavy content instead.

## What flops

- **A "SAT Math in 60 seconds" title with only one tip inside:** 92 to 7K views. A survey has to really cover a whole unit.
- **Desmos-only Shorts:** median 3.2K.
- **Weak survey topics:** data, sampling and studies, rates, area and volume.
- **"Day N" question series from small accounts:** 100-900 views.
- **Saturation:** "X% get it wrong" is heavily cloned. Dozens of copies sit under 2K, so the format only works when the math inside is genuinely surprising.
- **Sponsored posts:** views but almost no saves.

## Calendar

**Test dates.** College Board (V): Oct 3, Nov 7 and Dec 5, 2026, then Mar 6, May 1 and Jun 5, 2027. Aug and Sep dates are reported (R). The PSAT/NMSQT in-school window is Oct 1-30.

**Timing evidence:**
- Test-day posts spike.
- Prediction posts land a week or two before each test.
- Brainblast's multi-million run happened about two weeks before a March test.

**Cadence:**
- Predictions and surveys run from 14 to 3 days before a test.
- Trap questions run daily.
- On test day, post a recap of "the question everyone saw".

## Lessons for our style

1. **Hook with the problem, not the topic.** The exact question goes on the page in frame 1, read aloud.
2. **Show the wrong move first,** then strike it, then the one continuous transformation. This merges the trap format with "why a memorized fact is true".
3. **Prefer no-calculator, one-insight problems.** Non-SAT viewers can play along, and that is where reach comes from.
4. **Use surveys for saves and follows.** "Every SAT exponent rule in 60 seconds". A/B them against trap videos on Instagram, where surveys led the reference channel.
5. **Use true provenance, never fake prestige.**
6. **Remake winners.** Re-render proven problems with new numbers and across platforms. Proven problems recirculate across every account.
7. **Expect a power law.** Most videos land around 10-30K views. Judge formats by save and share rate as well as views, and cut losers fast.
8. **Supply tension visually.** A calm narrator has less built-in tension than the comedic "confused student" voices that top the niche. Make up for it with the strike-through, a visible timer, and a question in the first second.
