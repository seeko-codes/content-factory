## The production briefs

### DAY-20261029-01

**The two strips missing from (x + 4)²**

Advanced Math / Equivalent expressions

**Spoken hook:** Is (x + 4)² just x² + 16?

**First frame:** (x + 4)² = x² + 16? beside an outlined square with side x + 4.

**Viewer already knows:** Square area and multiplication.

**Promise:** See exactly where the cross term comes from.

**Proof:** Split each side into x and 4. Label the x² square and 16 square. Reveal two 4x strips, then combine them into 8x. Check x = 1: 25, not 17.

**Visual construction:** One square is partitioned in place. Only the two missing strips turn blue. Algebra follows the physical partition.

**Why this makes the slate:** A familiar error becomes an actual missing region. Strong broad entry and visible proof.

**Alternate angle considered:** Why does squaring a sum create three terms?

**Accuracy condition:** Physical side-length picture assumes x >= 0; distributivity extends the identity to every real x. One numerical check only disproves the wrong identity.

**Transfer check:** Expand (x + 2)². **Answer:** x² + 4x + 4.

**Idea-bank lineage:** ADV-001; SAT-020. The brief above governs this batch; some examples preserve the earlier construction and others rework it.

### DAY-20261029-04

**Find this without finding a or b**

Advanced Math / Equivalent exponential expressions

**Spoken hook:** If 3a + 2b = 4, can you find 8ᵃ × 4ᵇ?

**Proof:** Rewrite 8 as 2³ and 4 as 2². Then 8ᵃ4ᵇ = 2^(3a)2^(2b) = 2^(3a + 2b). Replace the whole exponent with 4; calculate 2⁴.

**Why this makes the slate:** A scary expression collapses through one visible structural match.

**Alternate angle considered:** Why do two unknowns still give one definite answer?

**Accuracy condition:** The product uses positive bases; no numerical values for a or b are determined or needed.

**Transfer check:** If 2p + q = 5, find 4ᵖ × 2ᑫ. **Answer:** 32.

### DAY-20261029-08

**Know the sum without solving either root**

Advanced Math / Quadratic coefficients and roots

**Spoken hook:** Find the sum of the solutions, without finding either one.

**First frame:** x² - 11x + 24 = 0; requested quantity r + s = ?.

**Viewer already knows:** Distributivity and roots as zero-producing factors.

**Promise:** Explain why the x coefficient reveals the sum for a monic quadratic.

**Proof:** Write (x - r)(x - s) and expand: x² - (r + s)x + rs. Match the x coefficient with -11x: r + s = 11. Briefly verify with roots 3 and 8 after earning the coefficient relationship.

**Transfer check:** For x² + 7x + 10 = 0, what is the sum of the roots? **Answer:** -7.

### DAY-20261029-12

Geometry and Trigonometry / Similarity and area

**Spoken hook:** Triple every side of this square. Does the area triple too?

**First frame:** A 2-by-2 square and a scaled outline with side 6; area multiplier = ?.

**Promise:** Distinguish length scale from area scale.

**Visual construction:** Keep the original square as a reusable tile. Fill the larger square in three rows of three identical tiles. Then show a triangle with matching base and perpendicular-height guides, both scaling by k.

**Why this makes the slate:** Countable visual surprise with a reusable dimensional rule.

**Alternate angle considered:** How many original squares fit inside the enlarged one?

**Accuracy condition:** The geometric objects must be similar and every corresponding length scaled. Changing one side alone is a different operation.

**Transfer check:** Similar triangles have corresponding lengths in ratio 2:5. Area ratio? **Answer:** 4:25.

### DAY-20261029-16

**“Given blue” changes the denominator**

Problem-Solving and Data Analysis / Conditional probability

**Spoken hook:** Why does given blue change the denominator?

**First frame:** Choose one of 12 shapes uniformly at random: 6 blue (3 triangles,3 circles) and 6 ink (1 triangle,5 circles). Given blue, P(triangle) = ?.

**Viewer already knows:** Probability as favorable outcomes divided by eligible outcomes.

**Promise:** Identify the eligible sample space in conditional probability.

**Proof:** All 12 shapes exist, but the condition says the chosen shape is blue. Dim the six ink shapes while preserving their outlines. Among six eligible blue shapes, count three triangles: 3/6 = 1/2.

**Visual construction:** Bracket the blue group as the new eligible whole. Use triangle/circle shape as well as blue/ink so meaning is not color alone.

**Why this makes the slate:** One word visibly changes the whole fraction; useful with low algebra prerequisites.

**Alternate angle considered:** Which objects are still possible after the condition?

**Accuracy condition:** Selection is uniform among the eligible shapes. Do not use 3/12 after conditioning, and do not imply the excluded objects disappeared from the original set.

**Transfer check:** Choose uniformly among shapes. There are 8 blue shapes, including 2 triangles. Given blue, probability of triangle? **Answer:** 1/4.

### DAY-20261029-19

**The value that makes the crossing disappear**

Algebra / Systems with no solution

**Spoken hook:** Which value of k makes these two equations have no solution?

**First frame:** 2x + ky = 6 and 4x + 6y = 15; no solution when k = ?.

**Viewer already knows:** Equivalent equations, line slope, and contradiction.

**Promise:** Distinguish parallel distinct lines from the same line.

**Proof:** Twice the first equation is 4x + 2ky = 12. For parallel lines its left side must match 4x + 6y, so 2k = 6 and k = 3. The constants then disagree: 12 versus 15. Subtraction gives 0 = 3, impossible. Thus k = 3 produces distinct parallel lines with no solution; for any other k, subtraction determines a unique y, then a unique x.

**Visual construction:** A line rotates toward parallel while the equation coefficients align. Finish on the explicit contradiction, then hold the two distinct parallel lines.

**Why this makes the slate:** A dramatic geometric endpoint paired with an exact algebraic reason.

**Alternate angle considered:** What turns a crossing into a contradiction?

**Accuracy condition:** A crossing leaving the screen does not prove no solution. If the constants were also proportional, the lines would coincide instead.

**Transfer check:** 2x + ky = 6 and 4x + 8y = 15: no-solution k? **Answer:** k = 4.

**Idea-bank lineage:** ALG-019; ALG-020. The brief above governs this batch; some examples preserve the earlier construction and others rework it.
