## The transferable 80/20

These are **plausible mechanisms and production hypotheses**, grounded in the execution above. They are not causal findings from the view counts.

1. **Choose a problem the intended viewer already feels.** Stalled reach and harder monetization are specific creator anxieties. For SAT, start with a recognizable wrong move or a question the learner can attempt immediately. Example: show `x² = 49` and ask for every solution, then reveal why one answer is missing. Keep the scope narrow enough to resolve in one reel.
2. **Make the promise and first demonstration agree.** The commentary states its disagreement immediately and puts old/new quantities on the board. Transfer that relationship: the first spoken line, first visible problem, and eventual proof should concern the same thing. A broad claim about improving a score needs stronger evidence than a single trick can supply. The guide's introductory explanation and early promotion are poor timing models for a 30-60 second lesson.
3. **Let each visual change advance the reasoning.** A/B/C arrows, view curves, and successive numbers make abstract talk inspectable. In the house style, use one persistent diagram: predict, change one thing, inspect the result, name the principle. Highlight the mathematical dependency exactly when the voice names it. The transferable unit is a meaningful state change, not a cut or effect every few seconds.
4. **Earn the surprise with something checkable.** The guide moves from a model to a real dashboard and from percentages to actual seconds. For SAT, expose the substitution, counterexample, graph, or arithmetic that proves the answer. End with a nearby case the learner can now solve. Confidence and contrarian wording cannot replace correctness.
5. **Improve from viewer behavior, within comparable lessons.** The creator recommends evaluating response as distribution expands at [00:12:09-00:12:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gk_3j1hvd6E&t=729s), and practicing through repeated short-form work at [00:39:53-00:40:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gk_3j1hvd6E&t=2393s). Treat this as advice to test. Compare similar topic, difficulty, duration, posting age, and audience; inspect where viewers leave relative to the first proof and CTA. Also check whether viewers can solve the follow-up. More views alone do not establish better teaching.

The guide's numeric retention targets, distribution theories, account-handling tactics, and posting-frequency rules are creator assertions. This two-video audit validates none of them. Keep the existing calm voice, restrained motion, and single CTA from [STYLE.md](../STYLE.md); test sharper problem selection, earlier mathematical payoff, and clearer proof before changing the visual identity.

## Method and limits

Used the installed `watch` skill against both public uploads, with no login or cookies. Read the full autogenerated timestamped captions for the guide; generated a local `whisper-cli` base.en transcript for the commentary after public subtitle retrieval failed. Automatic transcripts can mishear names and figures, so this analysis avoids depending on ambiguous wording.

Inspected every selected scene frame: 17 for the guide and 4 for the commentary, plus opening frames at 0, 3, 8, 15, and 30 seconds and distributed later frames. Additional Studio frames checked the evidence passage. Visual inspection was sampled, not continuous playback; subtle cuts, brief overlays, exact pacing, music, and sound design are not established by that method. Media, transcripts, and frames were temporary and are not repository assets. No private retention, click-through, watch-time, traffic-source, or conversion data was available. This audit provides reusable editorial hypotheses, not a statistically supported explanation of high-performing videos.
