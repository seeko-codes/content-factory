## Explanatory apparatus and asset decisions

The snowflake begins with one triangle and adds finer boundary protrusions as the iteration number advances from zero to five in six seconds. The original triangular mass stays visually evident. The ellipse demonstration keeps two red foci fixed, a blue point traveling along the ellipse and a green auxiliary construction point on a larger circle. Segments change while the ellipse and surrounding construction remain. This makes a dynamic geometric relationship inspectable, but the sampled image does not provide a clear numerical sum readout or a full proof of the title's constant-distance claim.

## Fit, failure modes and observation limits

Use the snowflake for a concise iteration/recursive-boundary reveal. Use the ellipse example as a starting reference for a dynamic locus construction with fixed anchors. For our output, make iteration labels, foci and the invariant directly readable instead of reproducing the small desktop-chart legend. What breaks the snowflake example is a transition that adds arbitrary spikes rather than the defined replacement rule, or a claim that five iterations depict an actual infinite boundary. What breaks the ellipse explanation is silently moving the foci or relying on a blue closed curve as the entire proof that summed distances are constant. This account's tiny current audience and legacy popular upload are descriptive facts, not an endorsement of the current channel's subject.
