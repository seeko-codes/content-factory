## [How to Take the Factorial of Any Number](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v_HeaeUUOnc)

Published 2022-08-13; 1591 seconds; 1,624,004 observed views on 2026-10-29. Selection: highest observed views in the bounded recent-tab sample. Review: sixteen timestamped frames, with sampled subtitle-text corroboration.

- **00:05-00:30:** A Desmos expression list and coordinate plane precede a factorial-like graph with repeated poles. The interface makes experimentation itself visible. The source is directly recognizable as Desmos here; this does not imply every later clean graph is a Desmos capture.
- **02:39:** A gamma-function integral is centered on black with a contrast between verifying and deriving it. The visual problem is explanatory motivation, not just evaluation. A remake should preserve why that expression is a candidate instead of dropping it in as an unexplained magic formula.
- **05:18:** The ordinary recurrence n-factorial equals (n minus one)-factorial times n is isolated. This supplies the discrete relationship that an extension should respect. It does not alone select a unique function on nonintegers, so the later constraints are mathematically important.
- **07:57:** A longer recursive product formula extends the one-step relation. The large central expression becomes the entire mathematical stage. Asset decision: maintain the indexing and product bounds clearly enough that the viewer can compare it to the preceding recurrence.
- **13:16:** A nearly linear graph segment, discrete cyan points, and a marked rise/run illustrate local behavior. The visual connects a large-argument approximation to an inspectable slope rather than a purely verbal limit claim. The sampled frame does not establish the full error bound.
- **15:55-18:34:** A logarithmic approximation leads to a yellow continuous curve through cyan sample points, with a limit expression nearby. The log representation makes multiplicative structure additive. Keeping the axes and which function is plotted explicit is essential because factorial and log-factorial have very different scales.
- **21:13-25:59:** An Euler portrait and historical date sit beside a product limit; later frames connect generalized harmonic notation, a derivative of log-factorial and the Euler-Mascheroni constant. These are related identities presented after the main extension, not evidence that any arbitrary interpolating curve would work.
- **26:30:** Nested white polygons fill black space. This geometric sign-off differs from the proof page and provides an unmistakable visual close. The exact rotation speed or audio accompanying it was not checked.

## [Extending the Riemann Zeta Function with Fractional Sums](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N0Ho0XGuXZs)

- **00:05-00:15:** The zeta series is introduced, then branched arrows connect it to a prime product, the unsolved Riemann hypothesis and the provocative negative-one/twelfth value. The unresolved label remains visible. The hook is a map of related claims, not a claimed proof of the hypothesis.
- **00:30:** A color-mapped plane displays only the right-side convergence region with a real-part-greater-than-one condition. This is a careful visual boundary. Extending the palette beyond that region later must represent a changed definition, not pretend the original infinite series converges everywhere.

- **07:28:** A continued function is labeled as analytic continuation, with a pole exception at one. The bright field is paired with exact wording about what was extended. A remake that simply spreads color across the plane without that qualification would change the claim.
- **09:58-12:27:** A decay condition and limiting difference of sums lead to a fractional-sum expression with a real-part bound. The construction introduces assumptions near its formula. The reviewer does not infer global validity of the limit outside those displayed conditions.
