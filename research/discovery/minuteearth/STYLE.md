# MinuteEarth: independent visual reference

Observed 2026-10-29. Scope: Two complete vertical Shorts: an 84-second animated sunset explanation and a 70-second outdoor presenter/dragon analogy about AI policy. These are deliberately preserved as different formats, not averaged into one channel look. English captions were available for both.

## Animated science mode
The sunset video uses uneven black outlines, small stick people, simple molecular symbols and large blank light-gray areas. Its beginning is a grid of visually rich sunsets with black landscape silhouettes. Later frames alternate between a hand-drawn microscopic explanation and a low-curvature horizon occupied by one tiny observer. The scale shift is structural: the same colorful light becomes a packet of wavelength lines near molecules and a long path toward the person.

Oxygen and nitrogen are rounded, lightly tinted two-lobed shapes with handwritten identifiers. Incoming light contains parallel rainbow-colored squiggles. Some short-wavelength strands divert away while warmer strands continue, so the physical selection is carried by paths rather than just a spoken color name. On the horizon stage, tiny particles remain visible around the beam. These are explanatory marks, not realistic atmospheric density.

## Layout, text and photographed material
Some comparison frames sit on a faint notebook-paper field with a red top rule. Side-by-side sunset panels separate the contribution of gas from the additional contribution of clouds. Handwritten-looking labels sit inside the panels rather than in a separate caption strip. The sunset photographs or photo-like cloud views are evidence of appearance, while the cartoon rays explain a proposed mechanism. Their textures should not be conflated.

The sampled frames do not show continuous burned-in narration captions. Exact font families and production software are unverified. Black silhouette boundaries, thin sketch marks, broad colored beams and warm cloud imagery coexist without a single fixed saturation level.

## Presenter/analogy mode
The second Short is a handheld outdoor selfie beside a lake. A man in a backward cap stays close to the camera; trees, sky, sunlight and lens flare remain in the live-action background. Flat cartoon dragons appear in corners and above him, followed by computer, environment, dog and legal-rule imagery. They are overlays on a real place rather than objects inside a physically coherent rendered environment.

The explanatory operation is a verbal analogy between attractive but difficult-to-control imaginary pets and AI. Cartoons change as the analogy advances; they do not demonstrate model performance or quantify harms. The final branded movement mark makes this an advocacy format. Its purpose, claim burden and ending are different from the sunset mechanism lesson.

## Motion, endings and failure conditions
Sunset frames progress through scattering, the longer horizon path and cloud illumination, ending on a saturated sunset image. The presenter clip stays mobile in one outdoor setting and finishes with its advocacy mark. Frame samples identify shot and overlay changes, not exact handheld movement or animation easing.

The sunset mode breaks if colorful rays cease to correspond to wavelength behavior, if cloud reflections are presented as the whole explanation, or if the miniature particles are mistaken for a calibrated simulation. The presenter mode breaks if the dragon story is treated as scientific proof about AI. Preserve the two formats separately: borrowing the notebook diagram does not imply borrowing the policy message, and the selfie format does not define the entire channel.
