# Welch Labs: inspected visual identity

Fresh visual observation: 2026-10-29. Scope is the specific clips in [breakdowns.md](breakdowns.md), not every upload.

## Appearance and variants

The Ptolemaic Short uses a black void, very fine neutral orbit curves, small planet-like colored bodies and a tiny white caption naming the model. The low ornament is intentional: accumulated paths are the content. The LeCun Short is a different documentary mode with a warm charcoal page, archival photography, off-white wireframe neural-network blocks and sentence-case captions on dark translucent boxes. Historical dates and model labels sit alongside the diagram. A channel-specific module must retain both modes; there is no evidence that Welch Labs always uses only orbital diagrams or always uses documentary subtitles.

## Explanatory apparatus and asset decisions

The orbital clip lets a moving body's path accumulate, then juxtaposes a simpler central model with the complex apparent one and connects the positions. The meaningful asset is the relationship between coordinate descriptions. In the LeCun excerpt, a camera-like move through a timeline of network architectures leads into an image -> network -> human label pipeline. Archival frames are used as historical evidence and the wireframe model becomes a repeated processing object. The two examples therefore need different asset packs: planets and trajectories versus archives, dated architecture diagrams and labeled image crops.

## Fit, failure modes and observation limits

Choose the orbital mode for coordinate frames, relative motion or parametric constructions; choose the documentary mode for the history of an idea where the archival material actually supports the narrative. What breaks the orbital example is an unlabeled frame switch or trails with no stable body identity. What breaks the documentary example is generic historical B-roll that does not relate to the named architecture, or a label flow so small that its semantic claim disappears. Only the first approximately 61 seconds of the 100-second LeCun Short were inspected in this pass; its ending and complete argument are unreviewed. The orbital model is a representation, not a historical or physical validation of Ptolemy.
