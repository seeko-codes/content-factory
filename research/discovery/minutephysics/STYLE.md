# minutephysics: independent visual reference

Observed 2026-10-29. Scope: Two complete vertical Shorts, a 19-second thermal-radiation explanation and a 60-second portal thought experiment. English subtitle text was checked alongside twelve sampled frames per clip.

## Page, layout and palette
The stage resembles light ruled notebook paper. Black drawings and handwriting carry most of the image; faint horizontal rules stay visible through large empty areas. The vertical composition keeps a compact diagram around the upper-middle portion, leaving substantial space instead of filling the phone with text. A candle, ice cube, stick person, sun and volcano are recognizable with very few marks.

Color is assigned to the physical referent. The heat clip uses red and yellow marks in flame and lava. In the portal clip, the entrance/exit pair stays orange and blue while pistons, arrows, labels and the stick figure are black. This is a drawing surface with selected colored objects, not a saturated backdrop. Neither reviewed clip uses live-action apparatus or rendered 3D materials.

## Mark-making and typography
The outlines visibly wobble and vary in weight like marker or pen strokes. Hatching marks solid cubes and piston parts; radial dashes indicate radiation. The text is handwritten in the same general visual vocabulary as the sketches, including temperatures, alternatives and section labels. An exact handwriting font cannot be established from the images. There is no continuous transcript ribbon or karaoke subtitle system burned into these sampled frames.

## Sequence and physical operation
The thermal clip begins with a glowing candle, broadens to cold and warm examples, then returns to a visibly hot material. The drawing permits invisible radiation to be represented through outward strokes rather than pretending an ordinary camera sees infrared. A humorous stick figure introduces a small character beat without displacing the explanatory objects.

The portal clip offers outcomes before replacing the original cube question with a piston mechanism. It preserves the orange/blue portals as a stable pair, then adds compression, force arrows and a throw/catch analogy. Across the sampled frames, the piston length, portal spacing and arrow directions carry the reasoning. Drawing appearance and state changes are observed; exact draw-on rates, stroke animation technique, easing and the sound mix were not measured.

## Variants, resolution and use limits
The 19-second item is a compressed concept statement. The longer Short is a multi-case mechanics argument with numbered options. Treat these as separate editorial scales within one drawing identity. The thermal item holds the volcano image through its end; the portal item ends with the pair separated around the piston, visibly answering its own hypothetical.

This style suits relationships that survive simplification to a few marks. It breaks when the sketch omits the assumption responsible for the result, when orange and blue exchange identities, or when a real measurement is implied by an expressive doodle. Handwriting should remain readable at phone size. The minimal scene makes every arrow unusually consequential, so an ambiguous force direction is a substantive failure rather than harmless decoration.
