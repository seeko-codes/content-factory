# VisualMath: observed execution

The verified creator is Daniel Tubbenhauer, whose channel is [VisualMath](https://www.youtube.com/@VisualMath). This name must not be conflated with other similarly named educational apps or social accounts. Both sampled videos are screen-recorded lectures with a small presenter inset in the upper-right corner. The main surface is a white PDF/slide or website window. Purple title boxes, thin horizontal rules, black serif mathematical text and occasional red-bordered images form the recurring document grammar. This is retained as an archived lecture reference, not an eligible mathematical-animation pair under the tightened scope.

[The AI-assisted mathematics lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9X0pKNxhSeA) alternates a historical slide sequence with live inspection of research papers and web pages. Historical photos, theorem-prover output, tables and a recent workflow chart are distinct evidence types. Colored bullet labels near the bottom distinguish achievement, method, ambition and next question. These bullets are not synchronized speech captions. A title card asks whether a machine can help formulate a theorem; the later source walk-through makes that question into a documented lecture rather than a free-floating prediction. The exact historical claims were not independently fact-checked for this gallery.

[The Lie-theory lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zit530rXWpQ) uses the same slide shell for a very different mathematical apparatus. Interactive-looking weight diagrams use triangular or square lattices, circles of different sizes, and a pale-purple chamber/quadrant. Later slides identify the torus action, integer weights and decomposition into weight spaces. This is not simply a pretty symmetry picture. Multiplicity, the action of a circle, and the distinction between one and several integer coordinates give the picture mathematical meaning. A faithful diagram would need those definitions and cannot replace the lattice with arbitrary particles.

The presenter is a small gray-haired figure against green in the corner, leaving most of the landscape frame to the document. Window chrome and occasional desktop interruptions remain visible. That is part of the observed capture method, not a recommendation for an active animation layout. Text becomes very small when a whole paper page or weight-diagram interface is shown, so a short portrait adaptation would require selecting one claim, one diagram and its key labels. A straight crop would remove either definitions or controls.

The endings use a green-highlighted thank-you line and a next-topic announcement. There is no recurring supporter-animation ending in the sampled pair. Subtitle excerpts corroborate spoken lecture discussion, including that some representation-theoretic claims are not fully shown, but no audio quality or exact narration cadence was reviewed. This channel's useful archived reference is the coupling of source documents to an organized technical lecture. The execution would fail if source pages were made unreadably small while carrying indispensable claims, if weight multiplicity were silently lost, or if the interactive diagrams were misrepresented as a bespoke continuously animated proof. Neither video qualifies as animation-primary on the sampled evidence.

## Evidence boundary

These are two-video, sixteen-frame-per-video sampled observations, not continuous playback. Intermediate motion is inferred only where successive states establish it. Exact timing curves, frame-accurate transitions, font identities and sound quality were not verified. Captions mentioned below are visible authored text; available subtitle text was used only to corroborate terms. The pair is intentionally contrasting and does not define every upload.
