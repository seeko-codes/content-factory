### DAY-20261029-V2-01

**A 45-degree line. A slope of five.**

Algebra / Slope and graph scales. Editorial confidence: **High**.

**Main question:** What is the slope of the line through (0,10) and (4,30), with the displayed unequal axis units?

**Spoken hook:** One square up. One square across. Why is the slope five?

**First frame:** A line through (0,10) and (4,30), drawn one grid cell up per cell across. x ticks read 0,2,4; y ticks read 10,20,30. Caption: slope = 1?

**Viewer already knows:** Coordinate axes and change in a quantity.

**Bounded promise:** Read slope in coordinate units, even when the picture tempts you to count squares.

**Visible proof sequence:** Trace the two-by-two-cell right triangle already on the line. Attach the endpoint coordinates without changing the grid. Its horizontal change is 4 and vertical change is 20. The quotient 20/4 is 5. Advance x by 2 along the same line: y advances by 10, confirming the ratio.

**Visual construction:** One persistent graph and one rise/run triangle. Cell counts become unit counts in place; the two axis labels remain visible through division. No corrective zoom or rescaling.

**Earned answer:** Slope = 5.

**Recognition cue:** Whenever graph-axis units differ, read coordinate changes before judging the angle.

**Conditions that must remain explicit:** A 45-degree line on the page need not have slope 1. Aspect ratio and coordinate units must be explicit.

**Transfer check:** A line passes through (1,4) and (3,10). Slope? **Answer:** 3.

**Reject or revise if:** Essential tick values are tiny, the scales change during the reveal, or counting cells is presented as a valid slope calculation.

**Why it earns a slot:** The eye supplies a tempting answer; the labeled units reveal a genuine discrepancy. **Alternate angle:** What do the squares actually measure?

**Remove a square. Reveal two factors.**

Advanced Math / Equivalent expressions and factoring. Editorial confidence: **High**.

**Main question:** Factor x²-9. Use x=8 as the opening area example.

**Spoken hook:** How does a square minus a square become a rectangle?

**Bounded promise:** See the side lengths that create the difference-of-squares factors.

**Visible proof sequence:** The remaining area is 64-9=55. Cut vertically below the removed corner: the left piece is 5 wide by 8 high; the lower-right piece is 3 wide by 5 high. **Recognition cue:** A difference of two squares factors into a difference times a sum.

**Conditions that must remain explicit:** The nondegenerate area construction uses x>3. Algebra proves the identity for all real x; x=3 is a zero-area boundary. Rotation preserves each piece, not its displayed orientation.

### DAY-20261029-V2-07

**Why 8% of 25 is the easy way around**

Problem-Solving and Data Analysis / Percentages and equivalent expressions. Editorial confidence: **High**.

**Main question:** Find 8% of 25, and explain why it equals 25% of 8.

**Spoken hook:** Eight percent of twenty-five. Try twenty-five percent of eight.

**First frame:** 8% of 25 = ? beside 25% of 8 = 2. A small equality question connects them; no unexplained instant answer.

**Viewer already knows:** Percent means per hundred; multiplication is commutative; 25% is one quarter.

**Bounded promise:** Understand when swapping a percent and its quantity preserves the result.

**Visible proof sequence:** Establish the swap on an easy 10-by-10 grid of 100 unit cells: the intersection of 20% of the columns and 50% of the rows covers 10 cells in either reading. Then write a% of b = (a/100)b = ab/100 = (b/100)a. Apply to 8% of 25: one quarter of 8 is 2.

**Visual construction:** The same 10 cells stay blue as the row/column reading switches. The grid demonstrates 20% of 50, not 8% of 25; make that transition explicit before applying the general algebra to the opening numbers.

**Earned answer:** 2; a% of b = b% of a because both equal ab/100.

**Recognition cue:** A single percent-of product can be swapped when the other percentage is easier to compute.

**Conditions that must remain explicit:** This identity concerns numerical percent-of calculations. It does not swap the roles of original and final amounts in percent change, or convert 8% of 25 into 25% more than 8. The grid is one example; algebra supplies generality.

**Transfer check:** Find 4% of 75 using the same swap. **Answer:** 3, because 75% of 4 is 3.

**Reject or revise if:** The grid is falsely labeled as the opening 8%-by-25 example, or the numerical swap is generalized to reversing a discount.

**Why it earns a slot:** The surprising shortcut has a countable rectangle and a one-line general reason, with very low algebra prerequisites. **Alternate angle:** One overlap. Two ways to read it.

**Principles:** P1; P3; P4; P6. **Reference evidence:** [reference 1](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/KpmrQdaQjGg); [reference 2](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/u0L7rvNumjw). **Main question:** For (x-3)²+(y+2)²=49, find the center and radius.

**Spoken hook:** Move this point anywhere on the circle. Which length refuses to change?

**Bounded promise:** Read a circle’s equation as fixed-distance geometry.

The signed coordinate differences are x-3 and y+2; their magnitudes are the leg lengths. Pythagoras gives (x-3)²+(y+2)²=r²=49. Read center (3,-2), radius 7. The equation means all points at that distance.

**Visual construction:** One fixed center and one tracing point. Horizontal/vertical guides change as the radius stays fixed. Signed coordinate differences appear in the equation; leg labels use absolute values when necessary.

**Earned answer:** Center (3,-2), radius 7.

**Recognition cue:** A sum of squared coordinate gaps equal to a positive constant describes fixed distance from a center.

**Conditions that must remain explicit:** Signed coordinate gaps may be negative but their squares give squared lengths. Radius is nonnegative; 49 is not the radius.

**Transfer check:** (x + 1)² + (y - 4)² = 36: center and radius? **Answer:** Center (-1,4), radius 6.

**Reject or revise if:** A signed gap is silently used as a negative physical length, the center moves, or 49 is called the radius.

**Spoken hook:** The square still needs the input one. Which x will give it one now?

**Bounded promise:** Explain horizontal translation through the function’s input.

**Visible proof sequence:** The original function sends input 1 to output 1. For the new function, the inner input must still equal 1: x-4=1, so x=5. Match (1,1) to (5,1) along a fixed horizontal guide. Repeat with input 0: x-4=0 gives x=4, so the vertex is (4,0). For every old input u, new x=u+4 feeds the same u into the square: every point shifts right four.

**Visual construction:** An input slot sits beside a single matched pair of points. Preserve output height while the x-coordinate changes. Only after both concrete matches does the complete curve translate.

**Earned answer:** The graph shifts 4 units right; its vertex is (4,0).

**Recognition cue:** To preserve the old output, set the new inner input equal to the old input.

**Conditions that must remain explicit:** Explain horizontal input shifts separately from a minus outside the function. The result is not a rule that every minus means right.

**Transfer check:** Where does y = (x + 2)² place the vertex? **Answer:** (-2, 0).

**Reject or revise if:** The curve moves before a correspondence is earned, axes move with it, or minus-inside is treated as a mnemonic without its input reason.
