## Variants, use and breakage

The graphing example ends with a green promotional panel and red arrow, while the Cramer example ends on the board setup within the sampled 60 seconds. The graphing clip therefore demonstrates a more explicit course/subscription ending; that should not be assigned to every upload. Automatic captions were read to disambiguate the original equation and the spoken column-replacement instruction. There was no independent listening, so speech warmth, room sound, pauses and background music remain unknown.

Use the graphing variant for a student who recognizes slope and needs to transfer it to the arms of an absolute-value graph. Use the matrix variant when the exact row-and-column placement is the lesson. This particular style breaks if the landscape insert becomes so small that minus signs and matrix entries cannot be distinguished. It also breaks mathematically if Cramer's rule is copied without requiring a nonzero coefficient determinant. An equation family in a large lower band can preserve orientation, but it cannot replace the example's actual parameters or a condition needed for validity. For a native portrait adaptation, recompose the board and teacher deliberately while preserving the observed teacher-to-work relationship rather than treating the black margins as essential mathematical content.
