## Direct review evidence

Review scope: 12 uniformly sampled timestamped frames from each locally processed video, plus English subtitle text for four. The two visual-only clips had no available subtitles. This is not continuous playback or an assessment of sound design, delivery, every transition or frame-accurate timing. Approximate times below follow the sampled media; subtitle clocks can differ slightly near the end. Smooth animations often returned only one default scene-change frame, so uniform sampling supplied the actual review coverage. No source media, transcripts or screenshots are adopted as production assets or stored in this repo.

### 1. Collision counts turn into digits of pi

[3Blue1Brown, 2023-12-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P11ykXwx4-k): 63,428,559 views, 60 seconds. A persistent simulation shows two blocks, a wall and a collision counter. Around [6 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P11ykXwx4-k&t=6s), a familiar equal-mass collision establishes the toy world. Around [19 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P11ykXwx4-k&t=19s), the count is three. The mass ratio increases to 100:1 around 22-25 seconds and the count reaches 31 around 33-35 seconds. At [38 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P11ykXwx4-k&t=38s), another mass increase leads to 314 around 45-47 seconds. The final sequence identifies the pi pattern and directs viewers to the longer explanation.

**Observed mechanism:** one comprehensible apparatus, one changing parameter, an accumulating counter, escalating numerical surprise. The diagram retains object identity instead of introducing new metaphors. This implements inspectable progress and low search burden.

**Theory correction:** the clip does not derive why the collision rule produces digits of pi. Finite displayed counts do not establish the general theorem or a pi limit. The payoff is a demonstrated phenomenon and an asserted pattern with a path to explanation. Therefore complete proof in every successful Short is not an immutable of reach. The stronger general principle is **honest scope and an earned payoff appropriate to the promise**. For our SAT lesson that promises to teach a method, sufficient reasoning and transfer remain acceptance requirements. For a discovery clip, the promised deliverable can be a remarkable result. Do not relabel an observation as a proof.

### 2. A familiar diagram is missing a necessary case

[Mathematical Visual Proofs, 2023-04-20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X-uesNEHv7U): 4,385,191 views, 57 seconds. The opening names an invalid four-set Venn representation. Around [6 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X-uesNEHv7U&t=6s), the viewer is asked what is missing. The replacement is built with tilted ellipses around 10-16 seconds. From [19 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X-uesNEHv7U&t=19s), highlights systematically verify exclusive single, pair and triple intersections, then all four and none, completing all 16 possible membership cases around 53 seconds.

**Observed mechanism:** recognition -> contradiction -> repair -> exhaustive check. Set notation stays attached to highlighted regions. The surprise comes from the mathematical requirement rather than camera motion. A useful SAT adaptation is to expose missing terms in a squared binomial or a lost solution after dividing by a variable, then explicitly check the repaired result. The source's relatively small notation is not an automatic mobile-legibility target for our work.

### 3. An infinite process becomes one visible fraction

[Mathematical Visual Proofs, 2023-03-16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u0L7rvNumjw): 4,289,934 views, 53 seconds. A regular heptagon is partitioned into an inner heptagon of one-eighth the total area and seven equal-area outer pieces. Around [10 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u0L7rvNumjw&t=10s), one outer trapezoid stands for 1/8. The same construction repeats inside the center, adding 1/8 squared and subsequent powers. Around [33 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u0L7rvNumjw&t=33s), the nested pieces form one extended region; around 42-50 seconds, sevenfold symmetry makes its total area one-seventh of the whole. The final equation connects the visible region to the sum.

**Observed mechanism:** consistent color/object correspondence, repetition with decreasing scale, then a global reinterpretation of the same object. This is a visual argument for a specific series, not a complete derivation of every geometric-series formula. SAT adaptation: a changed percentage base or area scale factor can use nested objects and a visible reference whole. Keep the mathematical domain and assumptions explicit.

### 4. Same creator and similar mathematics, typical reach

[Finite Geometric Sum Formula, 2023-04-18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Evbsk8yuoSQ): 44,391 views, 59 seconds, about 1.01 times the observed channel median. This is close in publication date to both winning MVP examples. At [0-11 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Evbsk8yuoSQ&t=0s), the title, symbolic sum and three variable conditions precede the completed visual apparatus. Around 19-39 seconds, rectangles build terms of the series; around [42 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Evbsk8yuoSQ&t=42s), the whole rectangle is measured a second way, yielding the formula by 54-59 seconds.

This has a stable diagram, aligned labels, a genuine mathematical construction and a finished formula, yet vastly less reach than the heptagon example. The general-variable opening asks for more prerequisite understanding than a concrete 1/8 picture. That is a plausible concept-level distinction to test, not an identified cause. It directly rejects the claim that visual proof or a black background alone predicts exceptional reach.

### 5. Strong visual equivalence, recent low-reach comparator

[A Self Similar Sum (Powers of 3), 2026-10-06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j2h679nv_Us): 4,492 views, 30 seconds, visual-only review. At 0-11 seconds, recursively grouped red dots stand for powers of three. Around [16-24 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j2h679nv_Us&t=16s), colored copies compare the accumulated smaller groups with the next power. Around 27-30 seconds, the sum identity is boxed. Stable referents, color-coded equivalence and a visual proof are present. The opening has little explicit viewer question and ends in formal notation, but this upload is only 23 days old. ### 6. An old low-reach example with real visual reasoning

[Visual Proof Short: Alternating Sum of Squares II, 2021-08-04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7HWH_qCO2w): 6,462 views, 52 seconds, visual-only review. The 0-second sample is black, 4.7 seconds shows creator initials, and 9.4 seconds shows the topic title. At [14 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7HWH_qCO2w&t=14s), the triangular-tiling premise appears. From 19-38 seconds, colored triangular strips embody alternating square differences; around 42-52 seconds, the assembled triangle and boxed identity supply the result.

This older example eliminates the simple explanation that every low-view comparator is merely new. It also contains stable animation and proof. Long branding/title setup, a less recognizable initial question, and very small formulas distinguish the sampled execution from the winner openings. These are hypotheses, with topic, era and distribution still confounded. A strong output standard can reject the opening for our intended audience without pretending the view count proves why it underperformed.

## Concepts this evidence makes worth testing

| Reusable concept | Relevant priorities | SAT implementation | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Familiar representation fails a visible check | 1, 2, 3, 4 | Show `(a+b)^2` as two squares, then reveal the two missing rectangles | Announcing a gotcha without making the missing relationship inspectable |
| One parameter changes while the apparatus stays stable | 2, 3, 5 | Slide a line's intercept while slope triangles retain the same rise/run | Changing scale and slope together so the viewer cannot isolate the reason |
| Same object, two valid measurements | 3, 4, 6 | Rectangle partition supplies both an expanded expression and a factored product | A decorative rearrangement whose dimensions are never labeled |
| Local repetition, global reinterpretation | 3, 4, 5 | Repeated percent changes visibly act on a changing base; compare the final whole | Treating additive percentages as multiplicative, or hiding the reference whole |
| Enumerate every necessary case | 3, 4, 6 | Recover the zero solution lost after dividing by `x`, then verify both roots | Testing a few cases and calling the result a universal proof |
| Concrete phenomenon before general notation | 1, 2, 5 | Show a 3-by-3 square growing to 4-by-4 before introducing a scale relation | A variable-condition lecture before the viewer knows the question |

These are domain implementations, not more immutables. The reviewed winner/control contrasts make audience recognition and the cost of entry credible hypotheses. They do not demonstrate predictive validity of all six priorities: there is no blinded preregistered score, balanced sample, controlled distribution or retention data here. Learning and transfer are not measured at all. A useful next test is to score a deliberately balanced set without revealing outcomes, then evaluate disagreements, rather than explaining every winner after seeing its count.

## What the research rules out

- A full proof is not necessary for every high-reach discovery Short. Promise type determines what counts as a complete payoff.
- A full proof, stable diagram, dark background or mathematical correctness is not sufficient for high reach. The same creator has all of these at typical and low view counts.
- A high view/subscriber ratio does not prove an editing technique caused discovery or that the video teaches effectively.
- A polished source's exact typography, opening duration and density are not automatically our output standard.
- There is no observed production choice here that guarantees low views. Wrong mathematics and false promises can still receive reach while failing our standards.

The actionable bar is to make a recognizable mathematical question, a coherent path and an earned bounded result visible. Treat public outperformance as a way to choose references and contrasts; treat the finished viewer experience as the quality decision.
