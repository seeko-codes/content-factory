## Appearance and variants

The sampled pair deliberately uses two different visual skins. Circle Illusion has a black background, a small white all-caps heading, electric-blue straight tracks and boundary, and warm orange moving points. Circle area uses a white page, black textbook-like labels, a blue/cyan filled disk and a softly shaded unwrapped form. Neither choice should be flattened into a universal channel palette. Both keep the main construction above large unused portions of the portrait canvas. The circle-area finish even includes a dense source-paper image: it is evidence/credit, not the same visual load as the preceding animation.

## Explanatory apparatus and asset decisions

Circle Illusion progressively adds diameter tracks and sliding orange markers until their positions trace a rotating-looking circular outline. The straight tracks remain the constraint, while the orange configuration supplies the surprise. The area video turns concentric material into an increasingly smooth triangular profile, then labels its perpendicular dimensions r and 2πr. Keeping r attached as the object changes is more important than copying the gradient. The stepwise-to-smooth transition is a limiting picture; it must not be described as a single finite rigid triangle dissection.

## Fit, failure modes and observation limits

Choose the dark line-motion variant for a constraint illusion that can be explained or investigated; choose the light unwrapping variant for a circle measurement where the circumference and radius become useful dimensions. What breaks these examples: hiding the straight tracks after the apparent circle appears, letting marker phases drift so the intended locus fails, claiming a finite number of rings literally has a perfect triangular outline, or leaving the r and 2πr dimensions ambiguous. For SAT, turn the inspection into the specific area or arc relationship the brief promises.
