### 2. A familiar diagram is missing a necessary case

[Mathematical Visual Proofs, 2023-04-20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X-uesNEHv7U): 4,385,191 views, 57 seconds. The opening names an invalid four-set Venn representation. Around [6 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X-uesNEHv7U&t=6s), the viewer is asked what is missing. The replacement is built with tilted ellipses around 10-16 seconds. From [19 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X-uesNEHv7U&t=19s), highlights systematically verify exclusive single, pair and triple intersections, then all four and none, completing all 16 possible membership cases around 53 seconds.

**Observed mechanism:** recognition -> contradiction -> repair -> exhaustive check. Set notation stays attached to highlighted regions. The surprise comes from the mathematical requirement rather than camera motion. A useful SAT adaptation is to expose missing terms in a squared binomial or a lost solution after dividing by a variable, then explicitly check the repaired result. The source's relatively small notation is not an automatic mobile-legibility target for our work.


### 3. An infinite process becomes one visible fraction

[Mathematical Visual Proofs, 2023-03-16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u0L7rvNumjw): 4,289,934 views, 53 seconds. A regular heptagon is partitioned into an inner heptagon of one-eighth the total area and seven equal-area outer pieces. Around [10 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u0L7rvNumjw&t=10s), one outer trapezoid stands for 1/8. The same construction repeats inside the center, adding 1/8 squared and subsequent powers. Around [33 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u0L7rvNumjw&t=33s), the nested pieces form one extended region; around 42-50 seconds, sevenfold symmetry makes its total area one-seventh of the whole. The final equation connects the visible region to the sum.

**Observed mechanism:** consistent color/object correspondence, repetition with decreasing scale, then a global reinterpretation of the same object. This is a visual argument for a specific series, not a complete derivation of every geometric-series formula. SAT adaptation: a changed percentage base or area scale factor can use nested objects and a visible reference whole. Keep the mathematical domain and assumptions explicit.


### 4. Same creator and similar mathematics, typical reach

[Finite Geometric Sum Formula, 2023-04-18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Evbsk8yuoSQ): 44,391 views, 59 seconds, about 1.01 times the observed channel median. This is close in publication date to both winning MVP examples. At [0-11 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Evbsk8yuoSQ&t=0s), the title, symbolic sum and three variable conditions precede the completed visual apparatus. Around 19-39 seconds, rectangles build terms of the series; around [42 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Evbsk8yuoSQ&t=42s), the whole rectangle is measured a second way, yielding the formula by 54-59 seconds.

This has a stable diagram, aligned labels, a genuine mathematical construction and a finished formula, yet vastly less reach than the heptagon example. The general-variable opening asks for more prerequisite understanding than a concrete 1/8 picture. That is a plausible concept-level distinction to test, not an identified cause. It directly rejects the claim that visual proof or a black background alone predicts exceptional reach.


### 5. Strong visual equivalence, recent low-reach comparator

[A Self Similar Sum (Powers of 3), 2026-10-06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j2h679nv_Us): 4,492 views, 30 seconds, visual-only review. At 0-11 seconds, recursively grouped red dots stand for powers of three. Around [16-24 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j2h679nv_Us&t=16s), colored copies compare the accumulated smaller groups with the next power. Around 27-30 seconds, the sum identity is boxed. Stable referents, color-coded equivalence and a visual proof are present. The opening has little explicit viewer question and ends in formal notation, but this upload is only 23 days old. Its view gap cannot responsibly be treated as a matched performance comparison to the 2023 winners.


### 6. An old low-reach example with real visual reasoning

[Visual Proof Short: Alternating Sum of Squares II, 2021-08-04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7HWH_qCO2w): 6,462 views, 52 seconds, visual-only review. The 0-second sample is black, 4.7 seconds shows creator initials, and 9.4 seconds shows the topic title. At [14 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7HWH_qCO2w&t=14s), the triangular-tiling premise appears. From 19-38 seconds, colored triangular strips embody alternating square differences; around 42-52 seconds, the assembled triangle and boxed identity supply the result.

This older example eliminates the simple explanation that every low-view comparator is merely new. It also contains stable animation and proof. Long branding/title setup, a less recognizable initial question, and very small formulas distinguish the sampled execution from the winner openings. These are hypotheses, with topic, era and distribution still confounded. A strong output standard can reject the opening for our intended audience without pretending the view count proves why it underperformed.
