## Appearance and variants

The inspected work is a black mathematical stage with crisp, thin white boundaries, serif mathematical notation and selective filled regions. In the Venn video, the plain top title changes from a warning that the familiar diagram is not a four-set Venn diagram to a claim that the replacement is. A white rectangle supplies the universal set. Colored ellipse outlines stay visible while one region at a time fills cyan, green, magenta or yellow. The sum video adds a large, light sans-serif title in its early frames, then lets the small notation and tall construction carry the explanation. This is not a single fixed typography treatment across every asset.

## Explanatory apparatus and asset decisions

The four-set construction depends on all membership combinations being available. Its elliptical outlines are not merely a pretty replacement for circles: region highlighting makes the logical distinctions inspectable. In the finite geometric sum, each green horizontal strip corresponds to a term, and a fixed width converts the stack into one rectangle. A gray ellipsis gap stands for omitted intermediate strips. The purple completed rectangle then becomes a compact expression for the same area. That transition is the asset decision: the viewer should be able to match the old stack to the final bounding dimensions.

## Fit, failure modes and observation limits

Choose this creator for set membership, dissection, tiling and algebra-as-area treatments in which labels can stay attached to pieces. Preserve the creator's willingness to leave the mathematical diagram alone on a sparse stage. What breaks this style: making four ordinary circles look like a complete four-set Venn diagram, filling several regions before the requested set expression can be located, animating a stacked area into a rectangle while silently changing width, or shrinking all labels to fit an unnecessarily tall crop. The sum's drawn positive areas assume a>0, x>1 and positive integer k in the visible statement. Broader algebraic identities require their own domain discussion. Source branding and exact fonts are not our assets.
