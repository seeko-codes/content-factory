## Mathematical reading and uncertainty

The first two formulas equal the real and imaginary components of (x+iy)² and (x+iy)³. That is an interpretation supported by the displayed algebra. The source does not name complex numbers on screen or explain angle multiplication and radial powers. Those facts must not be presented as if they were explicit visible labels.

A linear map preserves addition and scalar multiplication. Its images of lines are lines or degenerate points; it can shear or scale different directions differently. The public caption's broad contrast with uniform scaling should not be carried forward as a definition of linearity. The displayed nonlinear examples are enough to motivate bending a grid without that extra inaccurate restriction.

The formulas specify endpoints, but the sampled motion does not identify the homotopy used between the identity and each map. A visually smooth in-between image is not necessarily a particular one-parameter mathematical family. No input domain extent is labeled, so neither output distances nor apparent expansion can be read as calibrated magnitudes. Complex squaring/cubing are globally non-injective; attractive symmetric overlap does not demonstrate invertibility. The third map also has substantial collapse behavior, for example both coordinate axes map to the origin. A dense central pattern alone does not explain that property.

This is a strong reference for formula-linked changes in the shape of a whole coordinate field. It is a limited reference for tracking an individual point or proving a theorem about transformations. Its actual text collisions, unlabeled scale and three rapid case changes should remain visible in any evaluation of suitability.
