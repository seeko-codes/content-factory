# Effective educational videos: an evidence map

Observed: 2026-10-29. Scope: transferable principles for 60-second SAT math videos, including a connected-map opening that zooms into slope. Current SAT-001 is a four-domain survey: a 44-question hub connects four categories, then zooms into slope and the other three chapters. Its map edges convey category membership and orientation. The standalone slope examples below are future-video illustrations, not a proposed replacement for this survey. This is external primary evidence, not an audit of @dmscales or @dmstrategyy. House execution remains in [STYLE.md](../STYLE.md).

The useful 80/20: give the intended viewer a specific reason to care, create a question they can understand, make each explanatory step easy to follow, and deliver an answer they can use. These are design priorities, not a measured claim that a particular percentage of performance comes from them.

## What the evidence can establish

- **Platform description:** YouTube identifies signals and provides diagnostic guidance. This does not isolate the causal effect of any editing technique or reveal ranking weights.
- **Learning experiments:** Researchers manipulate instructional presentations and measure recall or transfer. This supports causal conclusions about those study conditions, not guaranteed reach on Shorts.
- **Curiosity research:** Loewenstein supplies a theory; later laboratory studies test specific predictions. Curiosity ratings, memory and information seeking are not interchangeable with feed retention.
- **Production inference:** Every implementation below is an adaptation to SAT Shorts. No cited experiment directly tests this project's animation, audience or opening.

## Seven high-leverage principles

### 1. Make the promise recognizable to the intended viewer

**Mechanism and evidence:** The viewer first has to identify why this is relevant. YouTube says Shorts ranking considers choosing to view, staying, and enjoyment, alongside personalization; topic interest, competition and seasonality also affect distribution. A successful format alone cannot explain reach. [YouTube Shorts guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en)

**Implement:** Make the promised task visible. For the current survey, the 44-question hub and four categories should establish what the viewer will learn about SAT math before the camera enters slope. A future standalone slope video could instead open with “Why can two different triangles give the same slope?” and immediately show the relevant picture.

**Failure mode:** A beautiful map whose labels, connections or destination are unclear. Broad spectacle attracts an audience whose question the lesson never answers.

**Measure:** Feed choice/stayed-to-watch, then retention through the first explanation, within comparable topic and traffic cohorts. For thumbnail surfaces, assess CTR separately. YouTube warns that CTR changes with audience and traffic source, and that narrower audiences can produce stronger CTR without wider reach. [CTR FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en)

### 2. Open a specific information gap the viewer can nearly close

**Mechanism and evidence:** Loewenstein's information-gap account proposes that curiosity arises when attention exposes a gap between known and desired knowledge. It is a theoretical account, not proof of a universal hook formula. [Loewenstein, 1994](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf) In numerical-fact learning, generating a prediction increased reported curiosity relative to comparison conditions. [Brod and Breitwieser, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0056-y)

**Implement:** Supply enough familiar information to make a prediction possible: show two slope triangles, ask whether their slopes differ, then reveal the invariant. Curiosity should concern the lesson's answer. A short mental prediction is optional; it must fit the viewer's prerequisites.

**Failure mode:** “Everything is connected” without a specific missing relationship; a mystery requiring knowledge the viewer lacks; repeated teasing after the answer is ready to show.

**Measure:** Ask a few target students what question they expect answered after the opening. Then compare continuation and whether they can state the answer afterward. Laboratory trivia research links higher curiosity with willingness to spend time/resources for answers and later memory, but it does not establish how long to delay a Shorts payoff. [Kang et al., 2009](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/WickCandleLearning.pdf)

### 3. Make the structure of the reasoning visible

**Mechanism and evidence:** Signals can guide selection and organization without adding subject matter. Mautone and Mayer found better transfer with structural signals in text, narration, and narrated-animation lessons about lift. Their tested signals included a preview, headings and causal pointer words. This is stronger evidence for signaling the explanation's structure than for any particular color or zoom. [Mautone and Mayer, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.93.2.377)

**Implement:** Give every beat one attention target and a clear role in the explanation. Use the house accent to identify the current object and words such as “because” and “so” when connecting claims. In the current category map, edges mean membership/orientation, not a causal or algebraic relationship between domains. For a future standalone slope lesson, the reasoning could be horizontal change -> vertical change -> ratio -> invariance under scaling.

**Failure mode:** Everything highlighted at once; animated edges with no stated meaning; a sequence of facts without the reason one follows another.

**Measure:** For an explanatory lesson, have a viewer explain why the result follows, not just repeat the formula. For the survey, check whether they can identify the current domain and what the map's highlighted edge means.

### 4. Put the explanation where and when its referent is visible

**Mechanism and evidence:** In controlled science lessons, nearby text and animation supported learning better than separated text and animation. [Moreno and Mayer, 1999](https://www.davidlewisphd.com/courses/EDD8121/readings/1999-MorenoMayer.pdf) In earlier mechanical-system experiments, concurrent narration and animation outperformed successive presentations on problem solving. [Mayer and Anderson, 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.84.4.444)

**Implement:** Draw the rise when the voice names the vertical change; put its number next to that segment. Keep the run visible while forming the ratio. Preserve recognizable objects through the map-to-graph transition so the viewer can track what changed.

**Failure mode:** Narrating a relationship before its objects appear; numbers far from their segments; moving the camera, labels and diagram while introducing a new formula.

**Measure:** Watch with target students and locate moments they lose the referent. Inspect those timestamps in retention, then test comprehension. Synchronization is not a requirement that every animation hit an exact spoken word: the 1999 study found no narration temporal-contiguity benefit when successive chunks were already short. Small timing differences and long separations are different claims.

### 5. Remove competing work while preserving the explanation

**Mechanism and evidence:** In four lightning-lesson experiments, adding interesting but irrelevant details reduced transfer; redundant concurrent text also impaired some learning outcomes. More material did not necessarily produce more understanding. [Mayer, Heiser and Lonn, 2001](https://www.csus.edu/indiv/e/eppersonm/hist107/documents/mayer%2C%20heiser%2C%20%26%20lonn%20%282001%29.pdf)

**Implement:** Fade irrelevant map branches once slope becomes the target. Remove stale intermediate labels, but retain values needed for the next inference. Let motion reveal a mathematical dependency. Keep house captions short and avoid adding a second paragraph or decorative visual stream beside them.

**Failure mode:** Treating visual activity as engagement; displaying every connection throughout; removing a needed premise in pursuit of minimalism. The experiments do not justify removing accessibility captions or assuming all text harms learning. Muted viewing and accessibility require separate checks.

**Measure:** Check whether viewers can find the needed quantity without searching. Compare comprehension with and without the suspected distraction; monitor retention as a separate outcome.

### 6. Pace by completed inferences, not by cuts per second

**Mechanism and evidence:** Mayer and Chandler found improved transfer, but not retention, when learners could control segments of narrated animation. The intervention included learner control and extra study time, so it does not prove that fixed pauses alone cause the benefit. [Mayer and Chandler, 2001](https://tecfa.unige.ch/tecfa/teaching/methodo/Mayer_Chandler01.pdf)

**Implement:** Finish one transformation, hold the resulting relationship long enough to inspect, then add the next dependency. A plausible slope sequence is picture -> count changes -> ratio -> scaled triangle -> same ratio -> SAT use. If it cannot fit legibly, reduce the lesson's scope before accelerating it. Exact beat lengths require testing.

**Failure mode:** Fitting an entire curriculum into the connected map; rapid changes that prevent integration; long pauses that contain no thinking task.

**Measure:** Observe where students need to pause or replay. Test a slower inference or smaller scope at that point. Replays alone do not distinguish delight from confusion.

### 7. Close the question with a usable result, then test both learning and viewing

**Mechanism and evidence:** Multimedia-learning research distinguishes recalling a lesson from applying its model to a new problem. The authors' cognitive-load framework treats transfer as evidence of meaningful learning. [Mayer and Moreno, 2003](https://carpentries.github.io/instructor-training/files/papers/mayer-reduce-cognitive-load-2003.pdf)

**Implement:** Deliver the promise made by that video's opening. In the current survey, give the viewer an intelligible account of the four domains and a concrete example of each, then close that organizing idea. In a future standalone slope lesson, resolve the opening comparison: “The triangle can change size. Rise divided by run stays the same.” Show its SAT consequence with a fresh pair of points or table. A map return should reinforce the organizing idea the video actually taught. Put a CTA after the promised answer.

**Failure mode:** A satisfying animation with no rule the viewer can reuse; formula recall without understanding; a CTA replacing the payoff. The CTA placement and map return are production hypotheses, not findings of the cited experiments.

**Measure:** Give an unseen near-transfer question and ask for a one-sentence reason. Separately inspect completion, post-payoff exits and useful audience feedback. YouTube explicitly notes that retention spikes may reflect either rewatching/sharing or unclear content that needed replay. [Audience-retention guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)

## Applying this to the connected-map -> slope opening

Keep the current connected-map concept as a candidate. Its defensible job is to orient attention to four SAT domains and locate each chapter. Its edges are category connections, not evidence of causal relationships among algebra, advanced math, data and geometry. The evidence does not establish that a map beats a direct opening, or that a zoom itself improves learning or reach.

Review it with four questions:

1. Can a first-time SAT student identify the 44-question whole and four categories without reading every node?
2. Is the current category and slope visibly selected, with the rest of the map receding as the first chapter begins?
3. Does the zoom retain the identity of the selected object and lead directly into a concrete change or comparison?
4. Does the ending deliver the four-domain orientation the opening promised?

If the map consumes time without answering those questions, simplify its labels, reduce the visible network or shorten the orientation. Compare it with a simpler four-category opening while holding the survey's explanation and payoff constant. Test a direct slope prediction separately when producing a standalone slope lesson.

## Practical measurement and limits

Use two scorecards: **viewing** (feed choice, retention by beat, average duration/percentage viewed, audience response) and **learning** (explanation plus unseen question). Optimizing one does not establish the other. YouTube names watch and satisfaction signals, but supplies no universal threshold or fixed ranking recipe. [Recommendation-system explanation](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)

**Keep denominators and dates explicit.** YouTube's August 2026 change counts public views from the first frame across formats, while most analytics remain anchored on engaged views. Historical public counts may therefore cross definition changes. [Official update](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/) For Shorts, engaged views exclude loops; stayed-to-watch is viewing versus swiping away; average duration and percentage viewed are calculated among viewers who stayed. A strong percentage viewed can coexist with many immediate swipes. [Current metric definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en-uk)

For a small production experiment, change one design variable, document the hypothesis before posting, and compare similar topics, lengths, traffic surfaces and observation windows. Organic uploads are not randomized experiments; audience allocation and competition can confound differences. Repeat a promising result before generalizing it. Do not infer causation from a few viral examples, copy raw view-count winners, or invent algorithm rules such as mandatory cuts, fixed hook seconds or a retention percentage that guarantees distribution.

YouTube's native title/thumbnail A/B tool currently excludes Shorts. An opening comparison here means a student pretest or cautious comparison across uploads, not a native randomized Shorts test. [Eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400)
