# Logic layer: the first principles behind our videos

This is the canonical logic layer: the underlying reasons for how we make videos, across subjects and formats. Its creator-strategy foundation is the DMScales study: [three algorithm explainers](research/dmscales-video-audit.md), [the remaining two main-channel uploads](research/dmscales-remaining-video-audit.md), and [both dmstrategyy uploads](research/dmstrategyy-video-audit.md). The full observations and their limits remain in those audits; learning research below supplies additional evidence for the educational commitments.

The [seven channel folders](research/channels/README.md) are the concepts layer: concrete manifestations of these principles. Their clip dossiers, reconstructions and audits are the details. Pair this logic with the [output quality bar](QUALITY.md), [house style](STYLE.md), and [revised 20-video slate](research/today-2026-10-29-v2.md). The governing idea is: **make a worthwhile question easy to enter, make its answer worth following, and make the central relationship possible to see**.

## What stays fixed, what remains a hypothesis

Our immutables are output commitments: the promise is honest, the representation is trustworthy, the viewer can follow the central relationship, and the payoff earns the attention requested. A lesson additionally owes valid reasoning and usable understanding. These are standards we choose to uphold across subjects, not claims that violating them guarantees low views.

The six priorities below are a mechanism-based creative model. Their effects on reach remain hypotheses. The [channel concepts and their details](research/channels/README.md) supply concrete executions and their limits; the [catalogue](research/math-animation-catalogue.md) supplies further discovery evidence and contrasts. A dissection, a map-first opening, paper backgrounds, voiceover and a particular edit rhythm are implementations, not immutables. Revise an implementation when evidence or the intended experience calls for it.

## From logic to the relevant channel

Start here before selecting a source. Propose who the video serves, what they should notice or understand, the promised payoff, and the visible relationship that can earn it. Agree on that intent with the owner through [the human decision checkpoints](README.md#human-decision-checkpoints), using a concrete recommendation and a likely channel reference. Once approved, choose the channel whose inspected work best embodies that intended experience. The channel is the concept; its specific clip studies are the details. Keep the full detail in its channel folder and load the relevant branch.

| Intended visual experience | Concept to open, then follow its links to the exact clip |
|---|---|
| A transformation or rearrangement makes a relationship visible | [Mathematisa](research/channels/mathematisa/README.md) for tile dissections and coordinate transformations; [Mathematical Visual Proofs](research/channels/mathematical-visual-proofs/README.md) for set regions and stepwise constructions |
| One moving object links simultaneous representations | [CodeMatrix](research/channels/codematrix/README.md) for shape-to-trace correspondence |

These are routing cues, not mandatory structures. Select an exact reviewed clip, preserve its observed behavior for a baseline, and record necessary subject adaptations. A useful source must fit the promised experience and the current campaign. Read [the channel index](research/channels/README.md) for scope and evidence limits. A candidate SAT brief does not select or approve its source format on its own.

## Four outcomes, four different questions

A good educational video has several jobs. They overlap, but one cannot stand in for another.

| Outcome | Viewer question | What helps | What would not establish it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choice | Is this for me, and why look now? | A recognizable problem, legible first object, specific credible promise | A polished diagram with no identifiable question |
| Continuation | Is the next moment worth the attention? | Progress toward the answer, meaningful uncertainty, relevant examples | Constant motion, or a question withheld indefinitely |
| Satisfaction | Was that worth my time? | An earned payoff, accurate expectations, useful or surprising resolution | Completion caused by confusion or an unresolved loop |
| Learning | Can I explain or use the relationship? | Familiar starting knowledge, checkable reasoning, a fresh application | Enjoying the animation, repeating the last formula, or saving the post |

YouTube describes choosing to view, average duration, percentage viewed and satisfaction signals in its Shorts guidance. These are platform observations, not a promise that a production technique causes distribution. [YouTube discovery guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en). Multimedia-learning research separately evaluates retention of information and transfer to new problems. [Mayer and Moreno, 2003](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_6).

Our working causal model is: audience fit makes the question matter; the opening makes it recognizable; a specific gap gives a reason to continue; visible reasoning makes progress understandable; the payoff rewards the attention; application makes the idea usable. This is a design model, not an estimated equation or a verified account of a recommendation algorithm. Topic demand, prior audience, competition and distribution can change reach even when the video is well made.

## The six priorities

### 1. Choose a question the intended viewer can care about

Topic selection precedes editing. “Quadratics” is a subject; “Why did dividing by x delete an answer?” is an understandable problem for someone who has solved equations. A useful topic has a real audience, a gap that audience can recognize, and a result worth knowing.

The opening does not need to dramatize catastrophe. Relevance can come from a mistake the viewer might make, a useful shortcut they cannot yet justify, an object they have seen without understanding, or a pattern that contradicts their expectation. A survey has a different promise: orientation to a clearly bounded collection. Both can work; the promise must match the format.

**Mechanism:** information-gap theory explains curiosity as a response to a perceived gap between available and desired knowledge. A gap requires some existing knowledge; incomprehensible novelty supplies little basis for a question. [Loewenstein, 1994](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75). Generating a prediction increased curiosity in a numerical-fact experiment, supporting a carefully chosen prediction prompt rather than a universal requirement to quiz viewers. [Brod and Breitwieser, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0056-y).

**Production consequence:** write the viewer, prior knowledge and missing answer before the title. Choose examples where a person can make a reasonable attempt. Prefer mathematical tension over manufactured social pressure. “Only geniuses solve this” and invented failure percentages do not improve the underlying question.

### 2. Make the opening a credible contract

The title or feed frame tells the viewer what this is; the opening supplies enough substance to make the promise believable. The proof and ending must deliver the same promise. An opening can demonstrate immediately, set up a prediction, or briefly frame a disagreement. Its job is to establish relevance and a reason to continue, not to satisfy a fixed second count.

For our short lessons, a concrete first frame is a strong default because it starts the viewer thinking with little setup. It remains a hypothesis to evaluate, not a law. A large visual map can create interest through breadth, but only if its organizing meaning is readable and its selected path leads somewhere useful.

**Observation:** the three short main-channel algorithm explainers start inside a familiar process. The two remaining main-channel videos instead spend roughly 13-16 seconds establishing a promotional or contrarian promise. The 48-minute second-channel guide has a roughly 33-second introduction. These are different executions; they do not establish one universally optimal opening. [Three-video audit](research/dmscales-video-audit.md), [remaining-video audit](research/dmscales-remaining-video-audit.md), [second-channel audit](research/dmstrategyy-video-audit.md).

**Production consequence:** someone seeing the first frame and hearing the first line should be able to describe the promised result. A title promising all SAT math cannot be fulfilled by a slope tip. A title promising a four-domain map can be fulfilled by an honest overview. Do not spend an opening on an identity introduction unless it adds necessary context for this viewer.

### 3. Turn uncertainty into inspectable progress

An intriguing question earns attention; a sequence of meaningful advances earns continued attention. Each beat should change what the viewer knows: establish a case, expose a failure, identify the missing relationship, prove it, or apply it. A cut, zoom or new caption is not automatically a new advance.

A useful narrative unit is **question -> attempt -> consequence -> explanation -> usable result**. It is a tool, not a mandatory template. A geometric derivation may instead be construction -> rearrangement -> invariant. A survey may be whole -> selected example -> relationship -> return to whole. Choose the structure that represents the subject truthfully.

**Mechanism:** structural signals can help learners select and organize an explanation. Controlled instructional studies support signaling the reasoning structure, rather than any particular color, pointer or editing rhythm. [Mautone and Mayer, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.93.2.377).

**Production consequence:** label the job of every beat in the storyboard. Replace “and then another fact” with the reason the next fact follows. Do not insert a new mystery whenever the previous one could be answered. The useful suspense is about the explanation, not whether we will eventually provide it.

### 4. Make the central visual carry the idea

The ideal visual does two jobs at once: it is interesting to watch and makes the central relationship inspectable. An expanded square reveals its missing strips; a denominator changes when the eligible group changes; a scale factor acts in two dimensions. In a lesson, the visual should carry the decisive reasoning. A phenomenon reveal can instead deliver a clearly bounded observation without proving its general cause. The promise determines which result is owed.

Start with something the viewer already owns, guide them to its principle, then apply that principle to the target problem. This is the education thread's [teaching method](../README.md#how-we-teach-extend-the-schema-the-learner-already-has). Naming a formula can follow the construction that made it intelligible.

**Evidence:** the studied creator repeatedly uses graphs, numerical comparisons and growing diagrams to embody his explanations. This is an observation about execution. A screenshot does not independently establish his causal interpretation of YouTube's internal processes. In our math videos, substitution, identities, counting and geometric construction can provide stronger direct verification of the actual claim. [Main-channel audit](research/dmscales-video-audit.md), [second-channel audit](research/dmstrategyy-video-audit.md).

**Production consequence:** identify the central object, the meaningful operation and what that operation establishes. For our SAT lessons, identify the proof-bearing object and the decisive argument as well. A counterexample can disprove a claimed identity, but does not prove the replacement identity for every value. A square-area picture assumes nonnegative side lengths; algebra extends the identity beyond that illustration. A few displayed cases do not establish a general theorem. Do not call a revealed numerical pattern a proof.

### 5. Spend the viewer's mental effort on the idea

Keep the current referent easy to find. Put a quantity near the segment, region or point it describes. Coordinate the spoken explanation with its visible objects. Remove stale material once it stops supporting the next inference, while retaining premises still needed to reason.

Distinguish information density from cognitive work. A sparse frame can be hard if it requires three hidden steps; a richer frame can be easy if its organization makes the steps obvious. Likewise, visual novelty can create a new burden if the viewer must keep learning a new analogy, coordinate system or color code.

**Mechanisms:** spatial contiguity, concurrent word/picture presentation, and coherence have experimental support in instructional settings. [Moreno and Mayer, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.91.2.358), [Mayer and Anderson, 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.84.4.444), [Mayer, Heiser and Lonn, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.93.1.187). These studies do not establish that accessibility captions are harmful or that tiny timing offsets cause failure. See the [evidence boundaries](research/video-principles-evidence.md).

**Production consequence:** one main attention target per beat is our default. Use restrained motion to show a change, preserve object identity through transitions, and hold the resulting relationship long enough to inspect. There is no defensible universal cuts-per-second target. Learner-controlled segmentation research also includes control and extra study time, so it cannot specify our fixed pause lengths. [Mayer and Chandler, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.93.2.390).

### 6. Deliver a result that survives the video

Resolve the promised question and leave the viewer with the result appropriate to that promise. A single-skill lesson should enable a nearby unseen example. A survey should leave a more useful organization of the subject. A visual reveal can leave a memorable, honestly represented phenomenon; it need not become a quiz or a complete lesson. Ask for the next action after providing the value.

**Mechanism:** a learner can remember a displayed answer without forming a model they can apply. Multimedia-learning research distinguishes recall from transfer. Curiosity studies support links to learning under their tested conditions, not a guaranteed effect of suspense on social reach. [Learning framework](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_6), [curiosity and memory](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x).

**Production consequence:** for lessons, write the transfer question and its correct answer when choosing the idea. Keep it as an internal quality check if including it would overcrowd the finished video. Other formats need a clear description of the promised payoff. An elegant loop must not conceal the resolution to induce another play. A CTA must not substitute for the payoff.

## What not to do

These patterns fail for different reasons. Wrong mathematics or an undelivered promise directly fails our output standard. Other patterns create avoidable risks to attention, trust or understanding. Neither category establishes guaranteed low views.

| Avoid | Why it weakens the result | Better output |
|---|---|---|
| A topic label instead of a viewer question | It supplies no particular gap or benefit to recognize. | A concrete, attemptable problem with a result worth knowing. |
| Inflated stakes, invented failure rates, or a title wider than the lesson | Attention is purchased with an expectation the explanation cannot justify. | A bounded promise whose exact result is visibly delivered. |
| Repeated teasers, delayed answers, and CTA before value | More viewing time is demanded without corresponding progress. | Early partial payoffs that build toward a completed explanation. |
| Reading formulas while skipping the decisive step | The student can copy the answer without knowing why or when it works. | A construction, comparison or argument that establishes the relationship. |
| Decorative spectacle or unrelated motion competing with a hard inference | The viewer must select and reconstruct the relevant information while also reasoning. | A striking visual whose change reveals the mathematical structure. |
| Labels far from their objects, disappearing premises, competing highlights | Searching and remembering consume effort needed for the inference. | Direct labels, stable referents and one clear attention target. |
| Accelerating a crowded script to hit a duration target | Shorter exposure does not remove prerequisite reasoning. | A narrower claim with time to inspect each important result. |
| A few successful examples presented as a universal proof | The evidence cannot support the advertised generality. | A valid general argument, or an explicitly limited example. |
| A loop, slogan or engagement request replacing the answer | The original question remains unresolved. | A useful conclusion and recognition cue before the next action. |
| Changing numbers to fill a quota | Repetition can produce more files without more audience value. | A slate with distinct questions, insights and visual constructions. |
| Copying visible features of winners as causal rules | Views mix topic, audience, age and distribution with creative execution. | Mechanism-based hypotheses tested against comparable outcomes. |

The paired acceptance criteria and concrete examples are in [QUALITY.md](QUALITY.md). A beautiful video can still fail the teaching; a correct explanation can still fail to earn attention. Both deserve revision.

## What the reference channels add

Their greatest value is a collection of concrete executions to inspect. Their public view counts can help select contrasts; they cannot tell us why an upload performed well.

The main channel returned five public uploads; its two largest algorithm explainers had about 700k and 363k views, while a similarly packaged TikTok explainer had about 29k. The two second-channel uploads had about 6.2k and 4k. This is a small, selected, age-confounded catalogue with different topics, audiences and durations. We have no private retention, traffic sources, impressions or launch-window results. The audits explicitly distinguish transcript-supported spoken content and sampled visuals from continuous playback or sound-design assessment.

The full catalogue also qualifies the initial pattern: the longer knowledge video uses substantial unrelated gameplay, and the idea video uses aggressive edits and a delayed-answer recommendation. These are creator choices, not universal standards. Our preference for visuals that explain is an educational design decision supported by learning research. We retain early partial payoffs and a final synthesis rather than concealing an answer. Audience-specific angles matter more than copying one opening format. [Remaining-video review](research/dmscales-remaining-video-audit.md).

Consequently, we adopt **bounded promises, visible reasoning, checkable examples and purposeful emphasis** as supported design hypotheses. We do not adopt hidden trust scores, account warming, numeric viral thresholds, future monetization claims or claims that editing caused the public counts. We preserve the reference math channel's useful visual craft through [STYLE.md](STYLE.md), without copying its branding or treating its strongest visible reels as a controlled experiment.

## How we improve the theory

### What the math-domain evidence changed

The [1,401-post discovery catalogue](research/math-animation-catalogue.md) includes fourteen sampled visual reviews, not fourteen hundred watched videos. It adds clear counterexamples to the stronger version of P4: the [63.4M-view collision Short](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/P11ykXwx4-k) reveals the pi pattern without proving its general cause; the [55.2M-view circular-motion Short](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/T4q5Sl8SYsc) presents emergence without a complete teaching sequence. A full proof, a spoken question and a transfer problem are not prerequisites for high public reach. We therefore broadened P4 from proof to inspectable central relationships and made P6 explicitly promise-relative. The SAT lesson bar retains decisive reasoning and transfer.

Conversely, the [44,391-view finite-sum proof](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Evbsk8yuoSQ) and [4.39M-view Venn replacement](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/X-uesNEHv7U) came from the same channel two days apart. Both contain stable visual reasoning. Explanatory craft is not sufficient for exceptional reach. The Venn entry offers a more concrete corrective opening; the sum begins with general notation and conditions. Lower prerequisite burden is a useful hypothesis, not a causal effect isolated by this comparison.

An [outcome-blinded eight-clip exercise](research/math-animation-principle-test.md) matched the observed within-group view order while ranking some stronger explanations below simpler reveals for reach. Equal six-criterion totals still accompanied very different counts. Selection knew the outcomes, the sample is small, ages differ, and only stills plus limited transcripts were reviewed. This is encouraging qualitative discrimination, not validated predictive accuracy. Do not convert a quality score into a view forecast.

### Tests we can actually interpret

Write a specific hypothesis before testing a change: “This opening should make the denominator change recognizable before calculation.” Identify both the viewing outcome and the understanding check. For published comparisons, use similar lessons, lengths, traffic contexts and observation windows, and repeat promising results. Organic uploads are not randomized experiments.

Diagnose the failure at the right level:

- If viewers cannot recognize the problem, improve topic framing or legibility.
- If the promise is clear but the middle loses them, inspect unresolved dependencies, slow progress or unnecessary detours.
- If they finish but cannot solve the nearby problem, improve the explanation or scope.
- If the math lands but response is weak, reconsider audience fit and the value of the promised result before adding effects.

Use chose-to-watch separately from engaged average duration/percentage viewed. YouTube's public-view definitions changed across formats in August 2026, so historical counters and engaged analytics require explicit denominators. [Current metric definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en-uk), [official update](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/). Retention spikes can reflect interest or confusion. [Retention guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en). Native title/thumbnail A/B testing excludes Shorts and does not test video openings. [Testing eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400).

The standard is an explainable improvement in the viewer's experience. Reach remains an outcome to observe, not something this theory guarantees.
