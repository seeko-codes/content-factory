# The output quality bar

This repository should let an agent produce videos that people choose to watch, find worth finishing, and remember for a reason. High volume means consistently meeting this bar across many distinct videos. The number exported is not a substitute for the quality of any one of them.

The bar below applies across subjects. The [SAT math profile](#current-profile-sat-math) adds the current educational requirements; [STYLE.md](STYLE.md) defines this series' visual and narration identity. A future content type can have a different profile and style while retaining the same standard of truthful promises, purposeful craft, and satisfying results. The [theory and evidence](LOGIC.md) explain our design choices; they do not guarantee distribution.

## What an excellent finished video delivers

Each dimension must pass. One exceptional animation cannot compensate for a false claim, and correct content alone cannot compensate for an unreadable, dull presentation.

| Dimension | The output we want | Reject or revise when |
|---|---|---|
| **A reason to care** | A specific question, experience, or result matters to an identifiable viewer. The angle makes its value apparent. | It is only a broad topic label, a generic fact collection, or a trend with no audience reason behind it. |
| **An opening with pull** | Title, first frame, and first line create one credible expectation. There is something concrete to notice, attempt, anticipate, or question. | The promise is vague, the image is only decoration, essential setup is missing, or the promise exceeds the payoff. |
| **Substance that earns trust** | Claims are accurate at the stated scope. Evidence supports the conclusion. Necessary conditions are present in the actual video. | The result is wrong, an example masquerades as proof, a source is misrepresented, or qualifications exist only in private notes. |
| **Visuals that do real work** | The central image makes a relationship, contrast, or experience more immediate than speech alone. Motion reveals something. | A formula is merely read over generic footage; a graph cannot be inspected; an impressive transition implies a false relationship. |
| **Progress worth following** | Each beat advances the question or deepens the experience. Important changes resolve into a result the viewer has time to inspect. | Facts accumulate without a relationship, the answer is delayed artificially, or speed forces the viewer to reconstruct missing steps. |
| **Deliberate visual craft** | Composition, typography, contrast, alignment, scale, and motion feel authored. Essential content reads at actual phone size and clears platform overlays. | Text clips, labels collide, numbers drift, emphasis competes, a needed object vanishes, or an obvious placeholder remains. |
| **Polished sound and captions** | Sound or deliberate silence suits the format. When speech is used, it is natural and intelligible, levels are comfortable, captions represent the final speech accurately, and meaningful visual changes land with their spoken referents. | Words clip, timing slips, captions race or contradict the voice, or production instructions are spoken aloud. |
| **An earned payoff** | The video delivers its opening promise and leaves a precise result, useful perspective, or memorable experience. Any CTA follows the value. | The ending is only a slogan, an engagement request, or a loop that hides the answer. |
| **A distinctive reason to exist** | At least one moment is specific to this idea: a revealing comparison, beautiful construction, useful insight, or unexpected but earned resolution. | Replacing the subject words would leave essentially the same video, or this is another batch item with different numbers and no additional value. |

“Wow” should come from what becomes visible or understandable. A large topic map can be a strong opening if the viewer sees its organization and the journey it promises. A dense wall of tiny topics fails even if the camera move is impressive. A simple missing rectangle can be a stronger reveal than an elaborate 3D scene.

**Keep the idea central without reducing its difficulty.** At each moment, every visible label, highlight, formula and motion should support the current reasoning or needed orientation. Clear material that has finished its job, including information that was relevant earlier. Retain premises the viewer still needs for the next inference. A hard concept can deserve a rich diagram; it does not deserve competing emphasis or obsolete working. Judge this through the sequence, not just a clean opening frame.

## Concrete anchors for the bar

| Weak output | Excellent direction |
|---|---|
| “SAT math is easy. Watch this.” followed by a slope formula. | An apparently slope-one line on a grid with unequal axis units. Read the actual rise and run, reveal slope 5, and explain why the visual guess failed. |
| `(x + 4)² = x² + 8x + 16` appears after a dramatic zoom. | The original square splits in place. Two overlooked strips become visible, each labeled `4x`. The `8x` term is earned by the picture. |
| “A 20% discount? Add 20% back.” | Eight retained tenths cost $80. Rebuilding the two missing tenths gives $100 and makes the percentage base inspectable. |
| A line rotates until its intersection leaves the frame, labeled “no solution.” | Coefficients become proportional while constants disagree. The visible `0 = 3` contradiction proves the claim; distinct parallel lines embody it. |
| “All SAT math in 60 seconds” ending after one trick. | A readable map of the four domains, honestly presented as orientation, with one useful example and a return to the whole. |
| Twenty copies of one trick with new numbers. | Twenty audience questions with varied mathematical relationships and visual constructions, each carrying its own insight. Shared craft makes them coherent without making them interchangeable. |

These anchors illustrate judgment, not mandatory templates. A new video can clear the bar through a different construction.

## What fails the bar

**Release blockers:** incorrect reasoning or answers; unsupported material claims; an undelivered promise; an essential missing inference; unreadable essential content; misleading visual transformations; or audio/caption defects that prevent understanding. Correct these before calling the video final. Source-dependent assertions must be traceable to a credible source and observation date where relevant.

**Creative failures that need revision:** an audience cannot tell why the topic matters; an opening could introduce any video; the middle makes no meaningful progress; effects compete with the subject; the ending does not reward attention; or the batch contains disposable duplicates. Revise the angle, construction, or scope rather than adding polish to an empty idea.

These are output rejection criteria. They do not mean a weak video cannot get views, or that an excellent one is guaranteed reach. The [failure-pattern explanations](LOGIC.md#what-not-to-do) distinguish design mechanisms from performance claims.

## Current profile: SAT math

The intended viewer is a student who recognizes the starting material but does not yet see the target relationship. Each lesson should leave them able to recognize when the idea applies and handle a nearby unseen example. An overview instead owes them useful orientation; it must be labeled and judged as an overview.

- **Correct at the actual scope.** Independently recompute the main answer and a nearby transfer answer. Check domains, units, signs, boundary cases, lost or added solutions, and required assumptions. A sample calculation can refute a universal claim; it cannot establish a replacement theorem. Distinguish evaluating `√25` from solving `x² = 25`.
- **A visible reason.** The student can explain the decisive step, beyond reciting the final formula. Begin with something the viewer already knows and extend it into the new relationship, following the [education thread's teaching principle](../README.md#how-we-teach-extend-the-schema-the-learner-already-has). Supply missing prerequisites or narrow the promise.
- **Honest SAT positioning.** Distinguish our SAT-style examples from official released questions. Keep original idea-bank lineage accurate. Invented error rates, score gains, frequency claims, and test-date urgency fail the trust standard. Official structure and changing test facts require a current College Board source.
- **Inspectability.** Relevant premises stay visible through the inference. Labels sit near their objects; units and denominators remain identifiable. Meaning is carried by position, shape, or labels as well as color. A mathematical morph preserves the claimed invariant; a navigational transition is recognizably navigation.
- **Transfer.** The brief includes a new nearby problem, its checked answer, and the condition that makes the method valid. The finished lesson supplies the recognition cue. The transfer question can remain internal if including it would overcrowd the reel.
- **A coherent current identity.** Use the printed-page style, measured typography, focused blue emphasis, and calm delivery in [STYLE.md](STYLE.md). Human narration uses the speaker's comfortable voice and pitch. A reference recording's pitch is descriptive, not a requirement for a human narrator.
- **Enough time to understand.** Short form is the present format, generally 30-60 seconds. Reduce scope before accelerating a proof. Fixed cut rates, compulsory prediction prompts, and a hook-duration formula are not evidence-based universal rules.

The [active slate](research/README.md) supplies the current candidate briefs. Their editorial rankings are recommendations, not predicted views. Existing videos are examples to inspect against this bar, not automatically approved gold standards.

## What counts as delivered

An idea request is complete with usable, checked briefs. A finished-video request is complete with a playable final export, its title/poster, and the source script/assets needed to reproduce it. The full current render must hold up in playback, with its intended audio when applicable, and at phone size; stills alone establish neither timing nor audio quality. Keep a short note of what was actually reviewed and any remaining limitation. A script, unfinished preview, or unchecked render must be labeled as such.

For a batch, every item must clear the same bar and the set must offer useful variety. Reuse components, visual vocabulary, and production knowledge to increase throughput. Preserve the insight and care that make each video worth watching. Execution commands live in [README.md](README.md), not in another mandatory process layer.

## Verify that the output follows the standards

When feedback changes a standard, verify that the actual exported video changes accordingly. Updating instructions, editing source code or completing a render does not establish compliance. This applies to every subsequent video for which the standard is relevant, alongside the existing output bar.

1. Read the current applicable standards here, in STYLE.md and in the selected source recipe or video brief. Record new feedback in its authoritative location. Translate it into an observable condition without weakening it or inventing a universal video structure.
2. Inspect the exact export being delivered against those conditions. For a revised video, revisit the original offending moment and check the rest for the same failure. Use playback for timing, transitions and sound; use frames when they can establish a visual condition. An author's claim or a few sampled frames cannot establish whole-video compliance.
3. Keep a brief review beside the export: identify the file and its SHA-256, the applicable standard/version, the observed evidence with timestamps or frames, and **pass, fail or unverified** for each applicable requirement. Explain exclusions. Evidence must show the condition, not just repeat the rule. A new export or changed standard requires a fresh relevant check.

For example, “clear stale labels” passes when the completed step's labels leave as the next step starts, while its needed premises remain visible. Show the relevant moments in the revised export and check every such handoff. “Removed the labels in code” is insufficient. “No competing emphasis” requires inspecting the whole explanation, not confirming the number of accent colors.

Use [the repo's verification commands](README.md#verify-standards-before-publishing) to bind the export and standards to the review, gate publishing and filter learning evidence. The verifier reports adherence and remaining failures. The existing production and learning loop owns fixes and instruction changes. A failed or unverified applicable requirement prevents calling the video final or publishing it as compliant. Before interpreting audience results as evidence about a rule, establish whether the published creative actually followed it; execution failures are not counterexamples to the rule.

This adopts the output-evidence principle from pstack's [Prove It Works](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/main/pstack/skills/principle-prove-it-works/SKILL.md), adapted to video. It does not require the rest of pstack or make a fixed editing template.
